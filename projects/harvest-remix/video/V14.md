## The globe recipe (why 11-petabytes works)

One three.js `Points` system of tens of thousands of small lit points (each a drawer lamp), drawn twice (sharp +
additive bloom), with ink silhouettes and hairline `LineSegments` for structure, one continuous camera move with a
huge change of scale (one lamp → a planet of lamps), and the type over it. One idea, one move, thousands of lights.
It renders fine under software WebGL. The four new hits below use the same recipe (shared helper extracted to
`assets/kit/hr-points.js`, 11 itself untouched).

## 1. Four globe-quality hits

**H1 · 03 "keep" → the vault (28.09–33.81, replaces the lit "keep" and the macro bolts).**
The meaning made literal: plan A (break it) is impossible, plan B is a shelf.
- On "keep" Archie, small at the left, slams an oversized rubber stamp **KEEP** onto the copy of the letter (the stamp
  is nearly as big as he is; the slam lifts him off his treads). The word "keep" *is* the stamp impression, in fire.
- He files it: the copy slides into drawer 0001, and the camera pulls back through the drawer front into a three.js
  **vault door** built from points and lines (hundreds of rivets and lamp points, line-drawn bolt housings).
- The seven-snare fill throws the door's seven radial bolts, one per snare, each lighting a ring of lamps; on 33.79
  the dial spins and the frame is the round door, which match-cuts into 04's clock.
- "you" stays the luggage tag on the copy, so "keep you" reads as *keep the copy of you*.

**H2 · 05 the harvest descent (57.68–61.35, replaces shots 6–7 and the crumpled vortex).**
- One continuous fall down the archive shaft, built as a points system: ~20,000 envelopes (instanced cards, each
  with a fire seal point) spiral down with the camera, and every one that lands lights its drawer lamp, so the shaft
  walls fill with light as we fall (the counter races alongside).
- No crumple: the stream thins to a single envelope (OBJ 0001) that slides into the last dark drawer; the drawer
  closes on the downbeat at 61.346 and its lamp is the first chip core of 06 (a match cut, not a hard cut).

**H3 · 21 the fridge, from below (131.02–134.51).**
- New angle: looking up into the dilution refrigerator from underneath, the classic chandelier view, as a line-drawn
  three.js object (plates as hairline rings, hundreds of coax lines as `LineSegments`, the qubit chip glowing at the
  centre). The camera rises slowly along it on "the fridge hums low".
- "the key falls out" without a cartoon key: on "key" the chip flashes and RSA-2048's two 1024-bit prime factors
  fall out of the bottom stage as two streams of digits (`p = …` / `q = …`, points that settle into lines of type),
  and `p × q = N ✓` stamps. That is what "the key falls out" means in Shor's terms.
- The "(oops)" marker comes back unclipped, beside the stamp.

**H4 · 23 the archive is read, and the ending (150.92–160.06, replaces the corridor).**
- Callback to the globe: the planet of lamps from 11 again (rebuilt with the shared helper). On "now" a wave sweeps
  across it and every orange "held" lamp turns cream ("read"), with the counter as `BYTES READ`; the camera pulls
  back until the whole planet glows cream (≈ 150.9–156.4).
- From the cream planet one point comes forward: the letter, OBJ 0001, opened, "no one else will ever read it."
  (≈ 156.4–158.2, as now).
- **The final ending:** on the 158.227 roll Archie, tiny, pulls the archive's light chain; the planet goes out row by
  row with the roll, leaving one lamp, and that lamp is the seal on 15's mail client. Then the bookend plays as is.

## 4. Text audit: every label must mean something

Rule: a text stays only if it (a) tells the story (who, when, what is happening), (b) is a joke, or (c) is a true
technical fact that fits the line. Codes that only look technical go.

- **Remove** (look technical, mean nothing): `DWG HN/DL-03 · SPLICE TRAY 3 · CAGE 12 · SHEET 3 OF ∞`,
  `ARAMID STRENGTH YARN` and the other fibre-layer callouts, `AISLE 00 · BAY 07 · TAP AMS-IX`, `INGEST AISLE 00`,
  `ARCHIVE RECORD · FORM HN/DL-01`, `SHEET 0001 OF ∞`, `NEAD ARCHIVE OPTICS · IRIS 07 · Ø 1104 MM · 9 BLADES · SER. 0001`,
  `PT HUM · 1.4 Hz · VIB · µg`, `Q-STACK · …`, `CIPHER BANK · 6 ROTORS`, the relay rack names in 02, and the repeated
  `OBJ 0001 · …` kicker on every scene (kept once, in 01).
- **Keep** (story, joke or fact): the TLS/X25519 plant and payoff, `CONSENT: IMPLIED`, `RETENTION: INDEFINITE · REASON:
  JUST IN CASE`, `≈ $0.001/GB·MONTH`, `PLAN A · BREAK IT`, `CLASSICAL ETA: LONGER THAN THE UNIVERSE`, `LOGICAL QUBITS:
  WORKING ON IT`, `SOURCE: EXPERTS (2015)`, `COPY FILED 2021 · UNAFFECTED BY PASSWORD CHANGE`, Mosca's card, `FIPS 203 ·
  ML-KEM · 13 AUG 2024`, the sprint board, the 4 K / 800 mK / 10 mK stage temperatures, `10 mK · COLDER THAN DEEP SPACE
  (2.7 K)`, `passwords changed ×14 · irrelevant`, `READ: NOW`, the SEC-0001 toast, `READ · 1 TIME`.
- **Replace** the kickers with one plain line where the scene needs orientation, e.g. 16 `the other end of the wire`,
  17 `the archive is watching`, 21 `the quantum computer`; or nothing.
- Every frame gets a per-scene list of its visible strings in `renders/texts-v14.md`, each tagged story / joke / fact,
  so the audit is checkable.
